Two apps, and why I bothered.

Enclosed Weekly Pulse  ·  PluckIt

Hello,

Dully was a school nickname that outlasted the school. It seemed friendlier on a title bar than my surname.

Dully Labs is the name I put on a very small studio in Hong Kong. Mostly it is a name for a habit — finishing things — and the whole staff is me: I write the code, draw the icons, answer the email, and keep the list short enough that the apps stay small on purpose.

The first one came out of a question I had been avoiding. Not how many hours I had worked; I knew that. How the week had actually gone. Whether work had quietly eaten everything else. Whether the people I love had been getting the leftovers of the day. Whether health was something I did or something I said I would start on Monday. So I made Weekly Pulse: four things — Work, Play, Love and Health — rated once a week, twenty seconds, with a note when there is something worth remembering. A few months in there is a line to look back along, and it is usually more honest than memory.

Those four are only the defaults. Rename them, recolour them, retire the ones that stop mattering.

What I care about most is what it does not do. No account, no server, no dashboard where I get to watch. Your check-ins stay on your device and sync, if you want them to, through your own iCloud. I cannot read them, and that is not a setting I added — it is the reason the app exists. Handing an honest answer about your own life to a company is a strange thing to have got used to.

The second app is smaller and more stubborn. I take a lot of screenshots — an address, a receipt, a slide somebody photographed from the back of a room — and getting the words back out of them nearly always meant sending the picture off to a stranger's computer first. For a job a Mac finishes by itself in about a second, that struck me as a poor trade. So PluckIt does it on the machine. Drop the image in, the text comes out, you copy it and get on with your day.

On-device, literally. Switch off the Wi-Fi and PluckIt carries on exactly the same.

Neither of them is trying to grow into a platform. They open fast, do one job, ask you for nothing, and I would like to keep them that way for as long as I am the one looking after them. If you try one and something is broken, or missing, or merely irritating, write to me — hello@dulangaj.com reaches me and nobody else, and I answer.

Thank you for reading the whole letter. That is rarer than it used to be.
